What is weave? from real transcripts

Utility, not features

It works for you. It interrupts you once. It asks before the irreversible thing.

Five moments from an ordinary day running weave — each one a real transcript from a live three-node fleet, not a mockup. The pattern underneath all five: you stop watching, and the fleet earns it.

$ weave tour — feel this whole page in five minutes, sandboxed, no API key

It noticed first.

A node's peer died while nobody was looking. Thirty minutes later — the threshold that separates a blip from a problem — the standing brief flagged it, named it, aged it, and said what to do. Nobody asked. Nobody was watching a dashboard. The judgment is a fold over the fleet's own log, so it can't miss what the log records.

           → weave peers shows the fleet by node; restart its unit on that host

Delegate, and forget.

One command creates a standing job the fleet owns — no cron, no unit file, no single machine whose death kills the schedule. Every window fires exactly once no matter how many peers race for it, every run is bounded by the budget you gave it, and the ledger attributes the cost by name.

Ask it what happened. It knows.

Every conversational turn is briefed with ground truth folded from the log at the moment you ask — counts, failures, spend, the fleet's health, and the notes you told it to keep. So the answer comes from what happened, not from what sounds plausible — even when the free local model is the one answering.

It asks before the irreversible thing.

Declare a task with --confirm and every irreversible call parks and asks — on the console as an Approve/Deny card, on any host's terminal, on your phone. And the rule under the rule: silence past the timeout is a no. An absent you is never an implicit yes.

One page per incident. Not sixty.

When something genuinely needs you, it reaches your channel once — with the remedy in the message. A standing problem stays quiet; a new one pages again. In the recorded run: two staged incidents, two messages, and twelve minutes of a standing alert delivering nothing more. Interruptions you can trust are interruptions you'll allow.
